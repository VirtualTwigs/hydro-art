The solo project · Hydro Art

Turn public river data into original, print-ready art.

Hydro Art is a reproducible GIS-to-SVG product: one command transforms authoritative hydrography into editable, museum-scale maps.

01 · The customer problem

Beautiful river art is typically manual, fragile, or legally ambiguous.

Creators

Need high-detail, original wall art without tracing another artist’s work or hand-editing GIS files.

Cartographers

Need clean, repeatable renderings from large, imperfect public datasets.

Designers

Need editable vector output—not a flattened image—so a map can become a print, product, or story.

The product is positioned at the intersection of creative tooling and trustworthy geospatial computation. The key differentiator is original-by-construction.

03 · How it works

A fixed 12-stage pipeline makes creative output repeatable.

Download→Extract→Validate→Repair geometry→Reproject→Clip→Build river graph→Compute watersheds→Assign colors→Generate SVG→Optimize→Export
Mention the important engineering choices: EPSG:5070 as a shared internal CRS; a directed river graph; deterministic watershed colors rather than random styling; and SVG groups by watershed.

04 · River-line math

Every channel width can follow measured discharge, not an arbitrary brush.

Log-normalized flow → stroke width

wᵢ = wₘᵢₙ + (wₘₐₓ − wₘᵢₙ) · tᵢγ

tᵢ = [ln(max(Qᵢ, ε)) − ln(Qₗₒ)] / [ln(Qₕᵢ) − ln(Qₗₒ)]

For each river segment, Q is NHDPlus EROM mean-annual discharge (QAMA, cfs). The logarithm accommodates roughly five orders of magnitude, keeping headwaters visible while letting main stems widen at tributary junctions.

Scale-aware art direction

  • State: log ramp, 0.35–3.5 width units (~10:1) for huge flow ranges.
  • Basin: shaped ramp, γ = 0.45, 0.35–2.1; expands readable mid-range channels.
  • Watershed: γ = 0.5, 0.35–1.4; aligns with the useful √Q hydraulic-geometry intuition.
  • Safeguards: nonpositive Q is floored at ε = 0.01; equal-flow networks render uniformly and deterministically.
Explain the formula plainly: calculate a bounded score for a segment’s flow, then map it into a print-safe stroke range. The log is essential at state scale. QAMA is a mean-annual flow measure, so it gives structural hierarchy; the seasonal reports use a different climate-driven disaggregation model.

05 · Print optimization

Preserve the master as vector; rasterize at the scale the print needs.

1. Author clean vectors

SVG keeps paths editable and resolution-independent. Optional SVGO compression runs behind a safe seam: if unavailable, the build delivers the original SVG rather than failing.

2. Tune for physical scale

Stroke widths are converted from projected map units to target pixels. Print presets enlarge strokes and prune/thin small waterbodies, points, and structures so they do not become ink noise.

3. Rasterize without renderer limits

Huge SVGs are split by watershed layer, rendered independently with resvg, then alpha-composited. This avoids the ~1,048,576-node renderer cap while retaining all detail.

06 · Jupyter reports · From art to insight

The same river network becomes an explainable watershed story.

Reproducible analytical narratives

  • Washington state, 1895–2025: a 131-year, statewide seasonal-flow reconstruction using public-domain NOAA nClimGrid data.
  • Hydrology beyond averages: flow-duration curves, center-of-timing drift, snow-versus-rain regime, analog years, and drought/flood record books.
  • Robust inference: Mann–Kendall trend verdicts and Sen’s slope rather than casual short-series extrapolation.
  • One shared recipe: notebooks call reusable report helpers instead of carrying their own forked statistics or plotting logic.

The reports state their limits: modeled seasonal behavior is not presented as a gauge record, inputs are rebuildable from snapshots, and validation compares like-for-like drainage areas.

This is worth showing if the interview values data products: the project turns a visual artifact into a decision-support narrative. Stress two practices: model limitations are explicit, and the analysis was corrected when the gauge was matched to the wrong drainage area.

07 · Architecture principles

Keep domain logic pure; isolate expensive reality at the boundary.

Dependency-injected seams

Downloaders, GIS readers, optimizers, exporters, and renderers are replaceable collaborators—not hidden globals.

Determinism by design

Equal inputs create equal outputs. Golden hashes and double-render checks turn “looks right” into a verifiable promise.

Honest product boundaries

Heavy GIS, network, and real-data jobs live in opt-in tools. The core stays fast and portable.

This is a good bridge to testing: the architecture was deliberately designed so correctness does not depend on a laptop having GDAL, a network connection, or multi-gigabyte data.

08 · Unit-test framework

Confidence without requiring a GIS workstation.

Test pyramid

  • pytest unit tests for pure configuration, graph, geometry policy, analytics, release, and fulfillment logic.
  • Offline integration tests run endpoint orchestrators against fake renderers and in-memory inputs.
  • Golden contracts pin e2e manifest/digest shapes and determinism behavior.
  • Node round-trip test keeps browser recipe logic portable and honest.

10 · Retrospective application

Retrospectives changed the next design decision—not just the documentation.

Observation

Offline tests could prove contracts, but not whether a human could directly invoke a newly packaged CLI.

Action

Added a uniform repository-path shim to direct-run tools and kept a real invocation harness outside the offline suite.

Principle carried forward

Separate pure contract logic in src/ from real artifact generation in tools/; validate both at the right level.
