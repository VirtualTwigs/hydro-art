Technical craft · an honest "how" behind the visual

Fine lines that survive the trip from map coordinates to a physical print.

Resolution-aware rendering—not a blind export

Source geometry is authored in a metric projected coordinate system, where a 0.35-meter stroke can vanish after a whole state is reduced to pixels. The rasterizer derives the correct document stroke from the actual output width:

units_per_pixel = viewBox_width / target_pixels
stroke_units = target_stroke_pixels × units_per_pixel

This means the target output—not a magic SVG default—sets the visual minimum. Per-river flow widths are also rescaled so their relative hierarchy remains visible at print scale.

Reports with methodological substance

  • Hydrograph timing: flow-weighted center of timing, then Sen’s slope plus a Mann–Kendall trend verdict—not a casual line fit.
  • Regime diagnosis: snowmelt fraction separates snowmelt, transitional, and rain-dominated rivers; melt-pulse drift is reported in days per decade.
  • Historical context: analog-year ranking compares seasonal shape; record books rank drought summer lows and flood peaks against the full record.
  • Distribution, not just averages: decade flow-duration curves show how the whole monthly-flow distribution shifts.

Inputs are snapshotted for rebuilds, model-vs-gauge checks are reach-matched, and reports label modeled flow as modeled—not observed discharge.

Next portfolio opportunities · built from current system capabilities

What to create next.

“River at Home” custom-print series

Make five county triptychs—river map, local watershed label, and seasonal-flow panel. It tells a clearer consumer story than a state map alone and proves personalization.

Watershed annual report

Create a 6–8 page visual report: basin map, 131-year seasonal comparison, peak-timing trend, flow-duration curves, validation, and an explicit methods/limits page.

Climate-memory motion reel

Produce a 20–30 second sequence: a state-scale annual cycle, then a zoom to one creek contrasting a wet and dry year. Ideal for a landing-page hero or social proof.

Infrastructure edition

Curate a “working water” print: river network with dams, gages, canals, intakes, and locks. Position it for civic, industrial, and museum audiences.

Terrain / river diptych

Pair a clean vector river print with a shaded-relief terrain version of the same place. The side-by-side makes the pipeline’s range immediately obvious.

Conservation field brief

Create a concise, honest report for one real watershed: seasonal low-flow risk, historical context, gauge comparison, data provenance, and actions—not a generic dashboard.
