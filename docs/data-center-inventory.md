## 2. Public Data Sources for Data Center Locations

### 2.1 EPA Facility Registry Service (FRS) -- NAICS 518210

- The EPA FRS is a centrally managed database of facilities of environmental interest
- NAICS 518210 = "Data Processing, Hosting, and Related Services"
- Queryable via [FRS Query](https://www.epa.gov/frs/frs-query) or [FRS EZ Query](https://epa.gov/frs/frs-ez-query)
- Bulk CSV downloads available by state, include SIC/NAICS codes, lat/lon, and cross-references to NPDES permit IDs
- **Action item:** Download all FRS records with NAICS 518210, cross-reference against NPDES water discharge permits

### 2.3 State Economic Development Incentive Databases

- **41 states offer data center tax incentives** (sales tax exemptions, property tax abatements)
- State economic development agencies maintain incentive agreement records, obtainable via FOIA/Open Records requests
- Key states with large incentive programs: Virginia, Iowa (100% sales/use tax abatement), Nevada (2% sales tax + 75% property tax abatement for 10-20 years), Minnesota ($30M threshold for 20-year exemption), Georgia, Texas, Ohio
- Look for: executed incentive agreements, economic impact analyses, correspondence with operators

### 2.4 Public Utility Commission Filings

- **FERC Docket RM26-4-000**: Federal rulemaking on large load (>20 MW) interconnection to interstate transmission
- **Pennsylvania PUC**: Model interconnection tariff for large load customers (>50 MW individual or >100 MW aggregate), requires public queue transparency
- **Texas PUC (SB6)**: Formal standards for large load interconnection; proposed rules require proof of site control, $50k/MW financial security, study fees $100k-$300k, and water/air permit documentation
- **Action item:** PUC interconnection queues are a rich source -- large load applications filed with utilities reveal planned data centers before construction begins

### 2.5 Water-Specific Regulatory Records

- **NPDES permits**: Required for data centers discharging cooling water blowdown, stormwater. Ohio EPA drafted general NPDES permit (OHD000001) specifically for data center wastewater
- **State water withdrawal permits**: Required in most states for groundwater/surface water withdrawals above threshold volumes
- **Virginia**: New regulations (effective Jan 2027) require waterworks operators to report data center water usage separately from other commercial/industrial users
- **Monitoring requirements**: Biocides, corrosion inhibitors, surfactants (including PFAS/PFOAs)
- **Action item:** Query state NPDES databases for permits associated with NAICS 518210 or known operator names

### 2.6 Local Government Planning/Zoning Records

- Data center projects require special use permits, rezoning, site plan approvals
- County assessor/parcel databases link operator LLCs to property records
- Building permits filed with county/city contain project details and contractor info
- **datacenter.fyi** aggregates construction permits from public records

### Water-Specific Environmental Concerns

- **A medium data center uses up to 300,000 gallons/day**; large facilities up to **5 million gallons/day** (equivalent to a town of 10,000-50,000 people)
- **40% of US data centers are in areas of high or extreme water stress**
- All US data centers combined consumed ~449 million gallons/day in 2021 (0.14% of US total, but highly concentrated geographically)
- Discharge water contains **biocides, PFAS, heavy metals, and corrosion inhibitors** requiring NPDES permits
- Key water-stressed expansion areas: Phoenix AZ, Las Vegas NV, Central Texas (Edwards Aquifer), rural Virginia groundwater

## 5. Recommended Discovery Workflow

### Phase 1: Bulk Seed Assembly
1. Download **ATLAS dataset** from GitHub (8,818 US records with GPS coords)
2. Download **IM3 Open Source Data Center Atlas** from PNNL (OSM-derived, enriched with county/state)
3. Download **EPA FRS** state CSV files, filter to NAICS 518210
4. Merge and deduplicate on lat/lon proximity + operator name normalization

### Phase 2: Water Permit Cross-Reference
1. Query state NPDES permit databases for known operator names + NAICS 518210
2. Query state water withdrawal permit databases for high-volume industrial permits in DC-heavy counties
3. Cross-reference against FRS registry IDs to link facilities to discharge permits
4. Virginia waterworks reporting data (when available Jan 2027) will provide direct DC water usage

### Phase 3: Enrichment from Regulatory Filings
1. PUC interconnection queue data (PA, TX, VA) for planned large-load customers
2. County assessor/parcel records for operator LLC property ownership
3. State economic development incentive agreements via FOIA
4. Local zoning/planning approvals for special use permits

### Phase 4: Ongoing Monitoring
1. Track datacenter.fyi for new construction permits
2. Monitor PUC interconnection queues for new large-load applications
3. Track state DEQ/EPA permit issuance for water-related permits
4. Monitor Kadoa operator database for new facility announcements
